What a runbook is

A live account workspace, not a document that gets emailed.

One runbook is one California department, owned by one named rep, behind a login, with an advisor inside it that answers from the filings. Built for a manufacturer, it shows where your product is, who is taking your ground, and which partner to move through. Built for a reseller, it shows your book, your renewals, and the whitespace beside them.

Hosted and gated

Per-user identity, verified against the signing keys. Not a shared password. A change to a play is traceable to the rep who caused it.

An advisor grounded in the filings

It answers from the runbook's own snapshot and can query the department's full purchase record. It refuses rather than estimates, and when it can't answer, that refusal is recorded.

A revision loop

A rep says what they learned in the field and the play rewrites itself, re-tiered honestly in both directions. What the rep said is stored beside what changed, and the history stays on the page.

A rep home page

Every account a rep owns, and every open action across all of them, rolled up by due date.

Every runbook shows the date it was built and the date it will next refresh. Point-in-time is a feature, not a caveat. A rep who is told the date trusts the number.
Why It Holds

Three claims, and how each one is enforced.

These are the things a technical buyer checks. Each is a property of the system, not a promise on a website.

01

Every number carries its filing

The purchase order number and its filed date sit next to the figure, on the page. Generation fails outright if a fact has no source attached. That is enforced in code, not by policy.

02

It refuses rather than approximates

The advisor does not estimate, and does not invent a number, date, name or title. Asked something the filings do not hold, it says so, and the refusal is logged, because the gap between what reps ask and what the data holds is the roadmap.

03

The corrections are the product

Every reseller can read the same public filings. What they cannot copy is a document corrected by the people who were in the room. When a rep says the renewal already moved, the play changes and the reason is kept.

The Evidence Rule

Three tiers. One rule.

A guess never wears the badge of a fact. Every finding is labeled by how certain it actually is.

Confirmed

On the record

A dated term sits directly on the purchase filing itself. No inference required. It's already written down.

Publicized

Vendor-confirmed

A manufacturer's own end-of-life or end-of-support bulletin, sourced and dated, even when it's not in the filing.

Inferred

Worth asking about

An absent purchase order is a question, not a finding. Old enough to warrant a call, and we hand you the question, never a date we can't stand behind.

"Never presented as a date. Always presented as a reason to call."
