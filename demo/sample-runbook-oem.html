What this rests on

  • "Yours" means purchase orders whose line text names a Palo Alto Networks product, whatever reseller carried them. The supplier on a California purchase order is the reseller, never the manufacturer.
  • Detected figures are floors. A purchase order whose lines say "backup software renewal" with no brand name is invisible to this method, and a product bought through a statewide vehicle may never appear at all.
  • Competition is derived from product layer, not from a maintained rival list: two products compete here when they sell into the same budget line, per the reference lexicon.
  • Spend is the purchase order grand total, counted once per purchase order. Line items are never summed — they double-count.
  • Absence of a purchase order is not absence of the product, and a quiet account is not a lost one. Every apparent lapse here is phrased as a question because the filings cannot answer it.

What it does not cover

  • This covers eSCPRS-reported state purchasing. Exempt state entities do not report to it, and business placed through other vehicles — NASPO, CMAS, an OEM's own SLP — may never appear.
  • All local government is outside this data entirely: counties, cities and school districts do not report to eSCPRS.
  • A purchase order's value is counted on the date the order currently carries, not as revenue is recognised. A multi-year order lands here in full on day one, so single years read high or low against an accounting system; lifetime totals are far closer.
  • Coverage before 2019 is thinner than after it for most departments.
  • Product detection reads purchase order line text against a maintained pattern list. Figures for any product are floors: orders that never name it are not counted, and cannot be.
