A buyer of record processes the paper. They are named on the filing, and they are usually not who chooses the product.

What this rests on

  • Spend is the purchase order grand total, counted once per purchase order. Line items are never summed — they double-count.
  • A filed end date is the state's own record. Where a purchase order carries the same start and end date, that is a delivery date and is excluded from every renewal figure.
  • Dates outside 1995 to fifteen years ahead are treated as filing errors and suppressed.
  • A purchase record is not an install base, and the supplier on a California purchase order is often the reseller, not the manufacturer.

What it does not cover

  • This covers eSCPRS-reported state purchasing. Exempt state entities do not report to it, and business placed through other vehicles — NASPO, CMAS, an OEM's own SLP — may never appear.
  • All local government is outside this data entirely: counties, cities and school districts do not report to eSCPRS.
  • A purchase order's value is counted on the date the order currently carries, not as revenue is recognised. A multi-year order lands here in full on day one, so single years read high or low against an accounting system; lifetime totals are far closer.
  • Coverage before 2019 is thinner than after it for most departments.
