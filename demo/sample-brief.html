Account strategy · 7 August 2026

Seat: Independent / consultant · Written by claude-sonnet-5 from 3,967 purchase orders totalling $1,562,659,590.

The angle

DMV's purchase record and its own hiring signals describe two different pictures of the same department, and nothing in the record suggests anyone is reconciling them. The clearest example: the single largest commitment in seventeen years of DMV purchasing — a $106,546,388 professional-services PO running 2026-05-18 to 2026-11-17 — is explicitly for migrating the mainframe off Endevor onto Changeman, while DMV's own job postings (independently, on calcareers.ca.gov) are still recruiting for staff fluent in Endevor SCM and the Broadcom/CA ESP scheduler. That is not a contradiction to sell against — it is a live, dollar-backed transition with no visible independent check on it. That is the wedge: DMV doesn't need another vendor, it needs someone who can tell it what its own $1.56B in purchasing actually says about where its risk and duplication sit.

Situation

The record covers 3,967 POs, $1,562,659,590, across 519 suppliers, from 2009-08-14 through contracts running to 2026-09-01 — 100.0% of recorded department spend by construction of the extract, but see limits below on what "recorded" means.

Spend by category, largest first:

  • Professional & Managed Services — 445 POs, $641,504,455, most recent 2026-07-24. Largest category by dollars, driven by a handful of mega-contracts rather than broad vendor spread.
  • Software & Licensing — 1,650 POs, $474,701,889, most recent 2026-09-01. Largest category by PO count by a wide margin — this is where the transactional sprawl lives.
  • Security & Identity — 227 POs, $160,112,769, most recent 2026-08-17. Dominated by identity-document production (IDEMIA), DMV's core mission function.
  • End User Computing — 548 POs, $87,840,213.
  • Print & Imaging — 386 POs, $47,700,642.
  • Support & Maintenance — 152 POs, $27,840,990.
  • Servers & Compute — 162 POs, $26,247,997.
  • Network Infrastructure — 70 POs, $24,908,565.
  • Non-IT / Facilities — 42 POs, $22,762,152.
  • Cloud & Hosting — 39 POs, $20,362,548.
  • Storage & Data Protection — 47 POs, $15,985,154.
  • Mobility & Telecom — 44 POs, $6,341,917.

Three things stand out across the survey. First, the top of the supplier list is not a competitive market — it's a small set of enormous, low-frequency awards: IDEMIA CIVIL IDENTITY NA LLC took $103,550,419 on a single PO (6.6% of all recorded spend), INTELLECTUAL TECHNOLOGY INC took $100,452,500 on two POs (6.4%), and DELOITTE CONSULTING LLP took $65,846,334 on two POs (4.2%). Contrast that with STRATO COMMUNICATIONS INC, the top supplier by dollars at $114,557,392 (7.3%) but spread across 100 POs — a genuinely active, ongoing relationship rather than a single legacy award. Second, Software & Licensing's 1,650 POs against $474,701,889 means the average transaction is small and the category is almost certainly fragmented across many budget holders and renewal cycles — the opposite problem from Professional & Managed Services. Third, at least one large program-level contract has already lapsed with no successor visible in this extract: DELOITTE CONSULTING LLP's $58,051,844 System Integrator engagement for the Vehicle Registration phase of the DxP program ran 2022-08-30 to 2025-10-01 — ended, on the record, with nothing named here picking up that scope.

Given the seat — no product, the analysis is the offer — the best ground is not the biggest category by dollars alone, but the categories where a reconciliation or governance question is both concrete and answerable: the mainframe modernization program inside Professional & Managed Services, and the licensing sprawl inside Software & Licensing, both of which have independent corroboration from DMV's own job postings.

Where to wedge in

1. The Endevor→Changeman mainframe migration — highest-value, time-boxed, actively running.

This is the largest PO in the dataset ($106,546,388, STRATO COMMUNICATIONS INC, 2026-05-18 to 2026-11-17) and it is corroborated, independently, by a DMV job posting seeking mainframe staff skilled in "Time Sharing Option (TSO)... Job Control Language (JCL)... CA/Broadcom ESP scheduler... Endevor SCM" (https://calcareers.ca.gov/CalHrPublic/Jobs/JobPostingPrint.aspx?jcid=254014). Two unrelated sources — a $106.5M contract award and a live recruiting ad — both point at the same legacy environment, at the same time it's being replaced. That is workable ground precisely because it is dated: the PO closes 2026-11-17, which means there is a near-term moment where DMV will need an independent read on whether the migration landed, what "done" means, and what happens to the staff being hired into the system that's being retired.

2. Software & Licensing sprawl, with Salesforce as the concrete entry point.

Software & Licensing is the single most transactionally fragmented category (1,650 POs, $474,701,889) — the kind of spend that accumulates duplicate seats, unmanaged renewals, and shadow ownership. Salesforce specifically shows up on both sides of the record independently: OUTREACH SOLUTIONS AS A holds $67,674,391 across 25 POs (4.3% of total spend) with a named $16,707,756 "Salesforce License Renewal" PO on the largest-PO list, and AGREEYA SOLUTIONS INC holds a "Salesforce Cloud Computing Implementation Services" contract ending 2026-08-15. Independently, two DMV job postings name Salesforce explicitly, one alongside MuleSoft (https://calcareers.ca.gov/CalHrPublic/Jobs/JobPosting.aspx?JobControlId=462422, https://calcareers.ca.gov/CalHrPublic/Jobs/JobPosting.aspx?JobControlId=517439). Multiple resellers, multiple contracts, one platform — that's a governance and true-up question a consultant can scope and answer without needing to sell anything into it.

3. The platform-to-purchase reconciliation gap itself, as a standing offer.

DMV's job postings name a long list of platforms — ServiceNow, Microsoft SharePoint Online/Power Platform, Azure AD (Entra ID)/M365, VMware, EMC SAN/Isilon, Brocade, Rubrik, AWS, and (as "working knowledge") Microsoft Dynamics, Oracle, Workday — that do not appear by name anywhere in the top-supplier or largest-PO lists in this record. That absence is not evidence the platforms aren't there — the suppliers on this list are resellers, and DMV very plausibly buys some of this through CDT or DGS statewide contracts that never surface in a department-level extract. But DMV itself may not have this mapped either, and "tell us what we're actually paying for the platforms we say we run, and through which vehicle" is a scoped, sellable, low-risk-to-commission engagement.

4. Infrastructure aging without a visible refresh — smaller dollars, concrete evidence.

A cluster of network and infrastructure line items has no renewal or expiration date past 2020–2022: PRESIDIO NETWORKED SOL GROUP's Cisco refresh ($11,704,148, dated 2020-04-07) and two further Cisco/data-center items ($2,051,923 in 2021-06-22 and $1,699,900 in 2022-06-29), NWN SOLUTIONS CORPORATION's UPS battery backup work ($3,374,328, 2022-06-30, and $1,099,370, 2022-06-26), CDW GOVERNMENT LLC's F5 appliance ($975,856, 2021-06-14), and TABORDA SOLUTIONS INC's Splunk subscription ($715,200, 2022-04-26). None of these prove the equipment is end-of-life — absence of a later PO is not absence of the asset — but it's a concrete, dated list to build a lifecycle-risk conversation around, and it's the smallest lift of the four.

Rank: (1) is the strongest — biggest dollar figure in the whole dataset, a hard date, and independent corroboration. (2) is close behind on corroboration strength and category-level fragmentation. (3) is the most "consultant-native" offer but harder to pre-sell without a specific hook. (4) is the easiest single meeting to get but the smallest stakes.

Questions to ask

1. The Endevor-to-Changeman migration ($106,546,388, running through 2026-11-17) is the largest commitment in your purchasing history — who inside DMV owns tracking whether it lands on scope and on schedule?

2. Deloitte's System Integrator contract for the Vehicle Registration phase of DxP ($58,051,844) ended 2025-10-01 — is that scope now covered internally, folded into another vendor's contract, or open?

3. Across the six people who sign your largest POs, is major modernization spend coordinated centrally, or does each buyer manage their own vendor relationships independently?

4. Software & Licensing is your largest category by transaction count (1,650 POs) — is there one owner of that spend, or is it distributed across budget holders with no single view?

5. Salesforce shows up under at least two separate suppliers and two independent job postings — is that one Salesforce org with one license structure, or several instances nobody's reconciled?

6. Your own job postings name ServiceNow, SharePoint/Power Platform, Azure AD, VMware, Rubrik, and Workday/Dynamics/Oracle — which of those run through CDT or DGS statewide contracts rather than your own purchase orders, and does anyone hold that full map?

7. Several network and infrastructure items (Cisco refresh, UPS batteries, an F5 appliance, a Splunk subscription) haven't shown a renewal since 2020–2022 — were they replaced through a vehicle that wouldn't appear here, or are they still in production past their planned life?

8. IDEMIA's card-production contract runs to 2028-08-23 — is there already a re-compete or renewal roadmap for that horizon, given its size?

Route to market

There's no reseller or manufacturer angle to place here — the seat has no product — but the record does show how DMV already buys independent advisory and staffing capacity, which is the likely shape of the consultant's own contract: PYXIS MGMT CONSULTING GRP LLC holds an "Organizational Change Management Services" PO worth $998,200 ending 2026-08-07, and E360 holds a "Staff Augmentation Professional Services" PO worth $999,678 ending 2026-08-14. Both are small, single-purpose professional-services awards under $1M — a plausible template for how a first engagement gets funded and scoped, and both are diarizable reference points for what DMV is willing to pay for outside expertise on a standalone basis.

What this rests on

  • Supplier names in this record are resellers of award, not manufacturers — claims about "what platform DMV runs" beyond the explicit PO description text are inference, not record.
  • This extract is DMV's own POs only; spend routed through CDT or DGS statewide contracts, or interagency agreements (one such agreement with the Dept of Human Resources appears in the dated-terms list), would not surface here, so category totals may understate true platform spend.
  • The record shows what was purchased, not what is currently installed or running — the aging infrastructure items with no renewal date past 2020–2022 may have been replaced through an invisible vehicle rather than left in place.
  • Job postings are an independent, present-tense hiring signal with no listed posting date in this extract — they confirm a current skills need, not a systems inventory, and their timing relative to specific POs (e.g., the Endevor/Changeman migration) can't be fixed precisely.
